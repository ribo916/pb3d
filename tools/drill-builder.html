How this works

Roster

2, 3, or 4 players. P1 (near) and P3 (far) are always present — the anchors; check or uncheck "include" under P2/P4 to add or remove them, as long as at least one player ends up on each side. Click a player's icon to select them for placement on the court. P1 & P2 are partners on the near side of the net (Team A); P3 & P4 are partners on the far side (Team B) — fixed by every game mode, not a per-drill choice. A shot in the Script panel always goes from a player to one of the opposing team's active players, the way a real rally shot crosses the net — never sideways to your own partner, so the receiver dropdown only offers opponents. Use "place landing" when the ball should land somewhere other than the receiver's current body position.

Positioning

Click anywhere on or around the court to place the selected player — not limited to a fixed grid, so serve positions and ATP/around-the-post spots well outside the sideline work too. The UI only lets you click on that player's own side of the net (near for P1/P2, far for P3/P4) — a click on the wrong side snaps to the nearest legal spot instead of being ignored. Wherever you place a receiver within their own side, the engine guarantees they're the one who receives that shot; a separate landing marker controls the ball path when needed. This positioning is Setup-only, before the rep begins.

Player directives

A player direction says: who moves, why, where, and by when. The coaching label (recover, shadow, crash, and so on) documents the intent for coaches and future editing; every label still uses the same real movement physics. “Arrive by” is operational: no deadline lets the player move normally, “ball bounce” paces them to this shot’s bounce, and “next contact” paces them to the next paddle touch. Click “set destination,” then click the court. The chosen direction remains summarized under the shot even when its editor is collapsed. Ball responsibility still takes priority if that player must play the next shot.
